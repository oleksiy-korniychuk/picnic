## 2026-09-30
The dynamic between the hideout upgrades and apprentice system will be very important. At the start of the game you won't have an apprentice so when you die you lose all character stat progress and all hide out progress. You will die a lot if it's your first time playing the game as you will not know how to avoid the various dangers in the zone yet. But as you as the player learns danger from opportunity you will survive, gain experience and make money enough to get bigger upgrades to your hide out. At this point dying becomes very painful as you lose all of your recently found progress, however around this same time your experience will attract potential apprentices. If you chose one they become a backup character with their own stats but a shared hideout. If your main character dies, you lose the stats progress but the apprentice inherits the hideout, carrying on your legacy (you then start playing as the apprentice). To work on the apprentice's stats you can choose to take them into the zone with you. This can be dangerous for them as they could die but they will acquire experience if they survive. Or you could send them in alone, even more risky depending on where you send them but they can gain experience faster if they survive and can provide a passive income as they can bring back loot from their excursions.

Addendum to the "hunch system" described earlier. Since the game is materializing to be about exploration and ultra hard without deep player knowledge, the hunch system should not be probability based but instead radius based with a "good perception" radius and a "peripheral perception" radius. This provides a much more me reliable perception system the player can reply on leaving the uncertainty to the world knowledge (anomalies, the map, items, events, etc). These radii can grow as a character's experience grows over time.

## 2026-07-03
The game loop and systems in Dave the Diver are a great inspiration for this game. I like how unlike how other extraction games, DtD has multiple locations you can go outside of the extraction area (The Boat, The Sushi Bar, the Fish Farm). I also really like the pacing and interweaving of story beats into these locations, adding to the continued sense of progress and breaking up the routine of the core game cycle (go in, extract, upgrade, repeat). These beats are also used as natural checkpoints to evolve the locations, mechanics, and switch up the gameplay cycle itself. This helps keep the challenge up but different as older mechanics are optimized out with QoL to be replaced by newer ones.

## 2025-12-04
To start out, if you die, everything other than the map knowledge resets. Once you unlock the stalker school upgrade for your base, your money and stash are now tied to the school and you get to pick which stalker to use once you die. You can also invest in the stalkers at your school to make them better for the inevitable moment when you must be succeeded. Plus they could provide some passive perks (resources, money).

## 2025-12-03
Character progression is as follows, going into the zone and making it out alive, improves a characters stats making future runs easier and possibly even unlocking perks. When your character dies, you must select another stalker from the local pool, none of their stats will be as good. However, if you are prudent, you can take a rookie stalker into the zone with you to "train" them, then when you die, you can choose them as their stats would be higher. Plus perhaps you can name a good rookie your heir which allows them to take your stash when you perish. A bit of a stretch goal here

## 2025-11-30
- The "Hunch" System:
    - Give the character passive skills like "Acoustics," "Thermodynamics," or "Intuition."
    - When adjacent to an invisible anomaly, the game rolls a check.
    - Success: The player gets a log message: "The hairs on your arm stand up" (Electro) or "The air smells like ozone" (Energy).
    - Partial Success: "You feel a sense of dread."
- Grid Distortion (ASCII/Tile VFX):
    - If an anomaly is present, don't show the anomaly. Instead, randomly change the color or character of the surrounding tiles every tew turns.
    - Example: A patch of grass (") near a "Witches Jelly" anomaly might flicker to a slightly different green or turn into a ? for one frame. This forces the player to watch the screen like a hawk.

## 2025-11-14
Single player RPG similar to NetHack but in a world inspired by Roadside Picnic. Your character is a stalker that goes into the zone to explore and find treasure to sell on the black market. You have to avoid detection, there can be a day and night cycle. It can have rogue lite elements like as you explore your map updates but sometimes events can cause the world to change leading to your map not reflecting reality. A core mechanic would be the anomalies and artifacts and how they interact with each other and with the mundane which is where complexity can come from.

A future feature twist (once the game is stable and fun) is that there is a meta AI that collects data about how players are playing the game, reasons about it, and then releases tweaks and updates to the game's rules to keep gameplay fresh

A game like this COULD work really well in a low graphics setting (top down, largely text based like dwarf fortress or rimworld) where the visuals come from text descriptions and the minds eye fills in fantastical details
